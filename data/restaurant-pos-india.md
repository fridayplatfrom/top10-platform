A café opening its first counter may need one till and a receipt printer. A cloud kitchen might need to route delivery orders for three brands to the same prep station. Both are buying a point-of-sale system, but their quotes should cover different work.

> **Start here.** Give each vendor your actual outlet setup and ask for two totals in rupees: what you’ll pay to get live and what you’ll pay to run the same setup in year two. For any feature you rely on during service, ask the vendor to demo it on the plan they’ve quoted.

**About the pricing.** This shortlist is based on public vendor pages. The fit assessments are editorial judgments based on those descriptions. We did not run hands-on tests or request individual quotes.

### 1. Petpooja POSS

Start here if your outlet needs billing, menu management and reporting in the same system. Petpooja shows kitchen displays and QR ordering among its add-ons. If your staff needs either on opening day, put it in the quote.

**During the demo:** Change the price of one menu item and ask the salesperson to show exactly which menus update. Then ask what the configured plan costs when billed monthly versus annually. [Petpooja pricing](https://www.petpooja.com/poss/pricing)

### 2. Gofrugal

Shortlist Gofrugal if the biggest problem is figuring out what the kitchen used, what it wasted and what to reorder. Its product page describes recipe management, inventory and production planning alongside POS workflows. The ₹500 starting figure is per user per month. Ask which user roles count and which modules are included at that price.

**During the demo:** Ring up a dish with an extra ingredient, void the order and inspect the stock movement after both steps. [Gofrugal restaurant POS](https://www.gofrugal.com/restaurant/restaurant-pos-software/)

### 3. Rista by DotPe

Rista is worth a look if you want tableside orders and kitchen tickets alongside a wider digital ordering setup. DotPe also offers separate storefront and QR solutions, so have the proposal name every product you’d be signing up for.

**During the demo:** Place a table order, change a side dish and split the bill. Ask for separate lines for POS, digital ordering and payment charges so you can compare each with alternatives. [DotPe product overview](https://dotpe.tech/)

### 4. LimeTray

Put LimeTray on the list if your restaurant takes orders online and wants to tie those orders to the counter workflow. Its POS page describes receipt and kitchen ticket printing and links to its order integration and customer tools.

**During the demo:** Put one item out of stock and watch where that change appears. Ask which connected ordering channels and marketing features the quoted subscription includes. [LimeTray restaurant POS](https://limetray.com/restaurant-pos)

### 5. Billberry

Billberry is a useful candidate when the central kitchen buys or prepares ingredients and distributes them to stores. Its product overview describes procurement, production and inventory workflows alongside the till.

**During the demo:** Transfer a batch to another outlet, have the receiving store record a short delivery and ask where both teams see the difference. Separate central-kitchen charges from each outlet’s charges. [Billberry product overview](https://www.billberrypos.com/)

### 6. Restroworks

Consider Restroworks if you manage separate outlets or brands and need to change some menu prices centrally while keeping local exceptions. The vendor describes central menu tools and says pricing changes with the scale and modules you choose.

**During the demo:** Update one shared menu item while leaving one outlet out of the change. Ask for the planned rollout cost, then ask for the cost of adding a location after launch. [Restroworks restaurant POS](https://www.restroworks.com/restaurant-pos-system/)

## Compare the same setup on every quote

Send vendors a short brief with your city, number of outlets, billing devices, kitchen stations, delivery channels, and the hours you need support. Ask each vendor to fill in the same four lines.

| Quote line | What to ask the vendor to include |
| --- | --- |
| Getting started | Hardware, menu setup, installation and staff training |
| Running the outlet | Licence, users, terminals, ordering connections and support |
| Charges that vary | Payment fees or any charge tied to order volume, an extra brand or an added outlet |
| Renewal | A twelve-month total for the same setup, including applicable taxes and any scheduled price increases |

## Run the test during a simulated rush

Give each vendor the same three orders from your menu. Start one dine-in order with a modifier, send a delivery order to the kitchen, and refund part of a takeaway order. Disconnect the internet before ringing up another bill. Check what the till and printers can do, then reconnect and look for missing or duplicate orders. Finish by exporting a day’s sales report and handing it to whoever reconciles the till.

### Do I need to replace my POS to bring delivery orders into one screen?

If billing already works well, price the integration on its own. UrbanPiper Hub, for example, connects online channels to a compatible existing POS. Ask about your exact setup and order volume before including it in a comparison. [UrbanPiper Hub India](https://www.urbanpiper.com/in/hub)

### What should I buy for my first café?

Write down what staff must do at the counter and in the kitchen every shift. Demo those tasks on the hardware you plan to use. If two systems pass, compare the first-year and renewal totals for that same setup and ask who answers the phone during your busiest hours.
